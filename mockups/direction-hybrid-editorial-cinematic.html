Alpine Dawn

The Story

Standing at the edge of the ridge before dawn, the world below was a sea of indigo shadows. Then the light came — not suddenly, but with the slow deliberation of something ancient and rehearsed. The peaks caught it first, turning rose and gold while the valleys still slept in blue.

This photograph was made in a single exposure at 2,847 metres, where the air is thin enough to taste clarity itself. The Hahnemühle Photo Rag paper was chosen for its ability to hold the subtle gradations of pre-dawn light — the way blue becomes lavender becomes gold in a seamless gradient that most papers simply cannot render.

"Every print is an edition of patience — the patience to wait for the right light, the patience to find the right paper, the patience to ensure every shadow holds its depth."

Each print is individually inspected, numbered, and signed. The certificate of authenticity includes the exact coordinates, time, and conditions of capture — a provenance as precise as the image itself.
